# Fluid dynamics

This folder contains the building blocks for a one-dimensional shallow-water simulation along the strip. Each cell represents a short section of the strip and stores water depth and discharge (depth × average velocity). The flux functions estimate how much mass and momentum move across cell boundaries.

## Files

- `PhysicsState.h` stores each cell's depth and discharge, plus the grid and simulation time.
- `PhysicsConfig.h` defines defaults for gravity, the CFL factor, the maximum time step, and the dry-depth threshold.
- `NumericalFlux.h` declares the mass and momentum flux type and the flux functions.
- `NumericalFlux.cpp` computes the physical flux for one cell and estimates the flux between two cells using HLL.
- `WaterSolver.h` declares the cell velocity helper.
- `WaterSolver.cpp` computes velocity as discharge divided by depth and returns zero for nonpositive depth.
- `BoundaryConditions.h` and `BoundaryConditions.cpp` are empty placeholders -
these will eventually handle the ends of the strip
- `FluidEngine.h` and `FluidEngine.cpp` are empty placeholders -
these will eventually handle the simulation loop
- `tests/placeholder.md` marks where the test suite will go.

## Physics and math

- Shallow-water equations: track how water depth and sideways flow change while conserving mass and momentum on a flat bed (will eventually figure out what to do for our curved bed, this is
just for now).
1. (https://www.clawpack.org/riemann_book/html/Introduction.html#an-example-the-dam-break-problem) 
2. (https://www.clawpack.org/riemann_book/html/Shallow_water.html)

- Rankine–Hugoniot condition: across a moving shock wave in water depth, the jump's speed times the change in the conserved quantity must equal the change in its flux to keep mass and momentum balanced across the jump 
1. (https://www.clawpack.org/riemann_book/html/Shallow_water.html#shock-waves)

- Harten–Lax–van Leer (HLL) Riemann solver: when two neighboring cells have different depths or velocities, HLL uses two estimated wavefront speeds and one intermediate state to approximate flow across their shared boundary (you can just read the first few paragraphs of the HLL section to get the idea and understand the code) 
1. (https://www.clawpack.org/riemann_book/html/Shallow_water_approximate.html#hll-solver)

- Courant–Friedrichs–Lewy (CFL) condition: for an explicit update that uses immediate neighbors, the time step has to be short enough that the fastest wave travels at most about one cell width per step (to prevent weird errors like we got with the particle gravity simulator with time steps!) read after Example 5.1.2
1. (https://tobydriscoll.net/fd-spectral-notes/advection/cfl.html#the-criterion)
